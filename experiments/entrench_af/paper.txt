## 7. Limitations & Ethics / Data Licensing Statement

1. **Observational Holter Cohorts:** IRIDIA-AF, SHDB-AF, and LTAFDB differ in clinical indication, monitoring duration (`24–72 h`), and sampling rate; fewer than `20` episodes remain at risk at `24 h` across cohorts (`19` pooled), precluding direct evaluation of multi-day pacemaker trajectories.
2. **Surface-ECG Surrogates:** Atrial fibrillatory rate and f-wave sample entropy are noninvasive surface surrogates of atrial organization rather than direct intracardiac electrograms.
3. **Right-Censoring and Eligibility Attrition:** Requiring artifact-free `≥5-min` pre-termination and uninterrupted `2-min` post-termination windows restricted complete-case marker regression from `2,346` qualifying terminations to `n = 336`–`449` events.
4. **Pacemaker Daily-Burden Emulation:** Emulating Spitaler et al. (ACaSA study: mode-switch burden `≥6 min/day`, `≥1,400 min` midnight bridging) on `24–72 h` Holters characterizes sub-day aggregation bias but cannot observe multi-month device episodes.

**Ethics & Data Licensing Statement:** This study is a secondary quantitative analysis of three publicly available, de-identified Holter ECG databases whose licenses explicitly authorize secondary research with attribution: **IRIDIA-AF** (CC BY 4.0, Zenodo `8405941`), **SHDB-AF** (ODC-By v1.0, PhysioNet), and **LTAFDB** (ODC-By v1.0, PhysioNet). No additional institutional review board (IRB) approval was required.

## 8. Structured Abstract A (Aims 1–3 — Termination Dynamics, Phenotypes, and Reinitiation Prediction)

**Title:** Early Duration Dependence or Recurrent Reinitiation Chains? Beat-Level Dynamics of Atrial Fibrillation Termination and Recurrence Across Three Holter Cohorts

**Background:** Continuous pacemaker monitoring indicates that spontaneous atrial fibrillation (AF) termination probability declines over 1–24 hours (91.1% at 1 h to 33.3% at 24 h; frailty Weibull shape $k = 0.767$), supporting progressive episode stabilization. Whether prolonged episodes observed with daily summaries reflect continuous fibrillation or frequent brief terminations followed by rapid reinitiation—and whether surface-ECG markers predict recurrence across cohorts—remains uncertain.

**Objective:** To characterize the time-course of AF termination rates at beat resolution, quantify how daily-burden episode rules aggregate short-gap recurrences, and test whether surface-ECG indices predict early recurrence.

**Methods:** Beat-level rhythm annotations were harmonized across 349 Holter recordings (307 AF patients; 4,425 episodes, 3,095 AF hours) from IRIDIA-AF, SHDB-AF, and LTAFDB. Time to spontaneous termination was evaluated using shared-gamma-frailty Weibull, piecewise-exponential, and kernel-smoothed Nelson–Aalen hazard models. Pacemaker daily-burden rules (`≥6 min/day`; midnight bridging across `≥1,400-min` days) were applied to quantify concealed terminations. Complete-case cohort-stratified, patient-clustered Cox models without imputation evaluated terminal atrial fibrillatory rate, f-wave sample entropy, and post-termination P-wave variability as predictors of recurrence, alongside within-patient vs. between-patient decomposition and leave-one-cohort-out external validation.

**Results:** Shared-frailty Weibull fits showed negative duration dependence across unfiltered episodes ($k = 0.566$ pooled; $k = 0.651$ in IRIDIA-AF). However, piecewise-exponential frailty hazards fell mainly during the initial minutes to hours—stabilizing by `10–30 min` in IRIDIA-AF (`0.356/h`, remaining `0.266–0.387/h` through `24 h`; left-truncated `≥60 min` shape $k = 1.013$) and by `1–2 h` in SHDB-AF and LTAFDB—with fewer than 20 Holter episodes remaining at risk by `24 h`. Among `≥1-h` daily-burden reconstructed episodes, short-gap (`<6 min`) reinitiation chains accounted for **5.1%** of AF duration in IRIDIA-AF, **54.2%** in SHDB-AF, and **81.2%** in LTAFDB. In `9` IRIDIA-AF patients (`19` repeat recordings), `<6-min` recurrence propensity showed high visit-to-visit stability (`ICC = 0.93`), whereas terminal fibrillatory rate (`ICC = 0.22`) and post-termination P-wave variability (`ICC = 0.04`) varied by recording. Patient-stratified Cox decomposition showed that longer episode duration did not reduce recurrence within patients across all `2,346` terminations (`HR 1.03, p = 0.46`) and primarily reflected between-patient differences. In complete-case regression (`n = 336`), surface-ECG markers were largely null, failed external cross-cohort discrimination (Harrell's C 0.480–0.491), and miscalibrated on LTAFDB (C = 0.587, calibration slope = 0.141).

**Conclusion:** In ambulatory Holter monitoring, declining AF termination hazard is concentrated in the first minutes to hours of episode onset, and prolonged daily-burden episodes frequently combine continuous AF with patients prone to rapid termination–reinitiation cycles that single-episode surface-ECG markers do not reliably predict across cohorts.

## 9. Structured Abstract B (Aims 2 & 4 — Episode Reconstruction Equivalence and Tail Distributions)

**Title:** Impact of Daily-Burden Episode Reconstruction on Concealed Atrial Fibrillation Terminations and Duration Distributions

**Background:** Pacemaker registries define atrial fibrillation (AF) episodes from daily burden summaries (`≥6 min/day`, `≥1,400 min` midnight bridging), whereas beat-resolved Holter recordings identify individual transitions to and from sinus rhythm.

**Objective:** To measure how daily-burden episode rules alter episode counts, conceal intermediate terminations, and affect heavy-tail vs. parametric distribution comparisons.

**Methods:** Across 349 annotated Holter recordings (IRIDIA-AF, SHDB-AF, LTAFDB), beat-level AF episodes across sinus-gap merging thresholds (`0` to `2,400 s`) were compared with daily-burden reconstructed episodes. Power-law (Clauset maximum-likelihood), lognormal, and Weibull models were compared via Vuong likelihood-ratio tests whenever tail sample size was `≥50`.

**Results:** Daily-burden rules compressed `4,266` completed beat-level episodes into `205` reconstructed episodes and obscured `1,980` internal terminations. Short-gap (`<6 min`) reinitiation chains accounted for **5.1%** (IRIDIA-AF), **54.2%** (SHDB-AF), and **81.2%** (LTAFDB) of AF time inside `≥1-h` reconstructed episodes. Across whole beat-level distributions, lognormal and Weibull models were strongly favored over power laws in every cohort (`p < 10⁻¹²`) and across pooled upper tails (`p = 1.4 × 10⁻⁵`). However, upper-tail Clauset fits on individual cohorts could not statistically exclude power-law scaling above `~2.1 h` in IRIDIA-AF (`p = 0.19–0.34`), `~57 min` in SHDB-AF (`p = 0.18`), or `~3 min` in LTAFDB (`p = 0.17`), while daily-burden aggregation left fewer than `50` tail episodes per cohort.

**Conclusion:** Daily-burden reconstruction masks cohort-dependent termination–reinitiation chains and reduces sample size for distribution tail testing; beat-level resolution is needed to separate continuous AF from clustered paroxysmal recurrences.
