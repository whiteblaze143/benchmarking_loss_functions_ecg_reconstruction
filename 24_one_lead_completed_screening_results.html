4.1 Evidence contract

This chapter freezes the interpretation of the completed three-epoch 1110000, seed-42 screening experiment. It is separate from the live queue, longer convergence extensions, spatial experiments, and external RDB evaluation. A completed screening row is not a replicated confirmatory result.

The primary registered chain is A0 raw → A0 + wavelet → A0 + wavelet + SSL. E1 is a complementarity test, not an improved baseline. Lead I and lead II are separate input contracts and are never averaged before showing their individual results.

4.7 What the registered contrasts actually show

The wavelet increment is the clearest short-screen effect. Relative to A0 raw, the wavelet branch without SSL increases wave mIoU by 0.079 on lead I and 0.043 on lead II, boundary F1 by 0.060 and 0.016, and P-wave IoU by 0.171 and 0.083. QRS IoU changes by only 0.001–0.003. This concentration on P/T rather than QRS is mechanistically plausible, but it is still an internal three-epoch result. The branch costs approximately 4.16 GB additional peak GPU memory.

The SSL increment is heterogeneous. On lead I it changes mean Pearson by −0.0005, lower-tail Pearson by −0.0041, and boundary F1 by −0.0011. On lead II it changes those endpoints by +0.0030, +0.0040, and +0.0358. It also adds about 8.45 GB beyond the wavelet-only model. The correct screen-level conclusion is therefore possible lead-II delineation complementarity, not a general SSL improvement.

Replacing Morlet phase with UEG phase changes mean Pearson by less than 0.0002 on either lead. Boundary F1 increases by 0.0030 on lead I and 0.0105 on lead II, but the lower-tail Pearson direction reverses between leads. This is too small and inconsistent for a physiology claim without longer matched training.

Only the lead-II E1 complementarity cell completed. It is essentially tied with A0+wavelet+SSL while using approximately 5.94 GB more memory. Because lead I is missing, this contrast is both single-seed and asymmetrically censored.

Mean Pearson and reconstruction loss are almost redundant in rank (absolute Spearman approximately 0.99), while boundary F1 correlates only about 0.11 with mean Pearson. Wave mIoU is moderately coupled to both. This explains why a reconstruction leader need not be a boundary leader and why a single composite score embeds a scientific value judgement.

With 115 completed rows and many endpoints, the metric-wise maximum is strongly subject to winner’s curse. A configuration appearing once at the top is a candidate, not a confirmed effect. The registered contrasts above are more interpretable than post-hoc leaderboard mining, even though they still lack replication.

4.9 Metric-Space Screening UMAP Manifolds

To map the high-dimensional performance landscape across all 115 completed screening configurations, we compute 2D and 3D non-linear UMAP manifolds over the standardized matrix of all available validation, delineation, loss, and hardware metrics:

## Registered mechanism contrasts

```{python}
#| label: completed-onelead-registered-contrasts
pairs = [
    ("wavelet increment", "A0_raw", "A0_wave_noSSL_gated_add"),
    ("SSL increment", "A0_wave_noSSL_gated_add", "ssl_magnitude_phase_local_gated_add"),
    ("physiological view", "R0_morlet_mag_morlet_phase", "R1_morlet_mag_ueg_phase"),
    ("E1 complementarity", "ssl_magnitude_phase_local_gated_add", "E1_ssl_local"),
]
rows = []
keys = ["seed", "observed_lead"]
for label, left_name, right_name in pairs:
    left = completed.loc[completed.cell_name.eq(left_name), keys + metrics]
    right = completed.loc[completed.cell_name.eq(right_name), keys + metrics]
    for _, row in left.merge(right, on=keys, suffixes=("_left", "_right")).iterrows():
        out = {"contrast": label, "left": left_name, "right": right_name,
               "seed": row.seed, "observed_lead": row.observed_lead}
        for metric in metrics:
            sign = -1 if metric_direction(metric) == "min" else 1
            out[f"improvement.{metric}"] = sign * (row[f"{metric}_right"] - row[f"{metric}_left"])
        rows.append(out)
display(pd.DataFrame(rows) if rows else pd.DataFrame([{"status": "contrast unavailable"}]))
```

These contrasts isolate reviewer-relevant questions, but the experiment has one
seed. They nominate configurations for convergence testing; they do not supply
uncertainty estimates or multiplicity-controlled confirmation.

## What the registered contrasts actually show

The wavelet increment is the clearest short-screen effect. Relative to A0 raw,
the wavelet branch without SSL increases wave mIoU by 0.079 on lead I and 0.043
on lead II, boundary F1 by 0.060 and 0.016, and P-wave IoU by 0.171 and 0.083.
QRS IoU changes by only 0.001–0.003. This concentration on P/T rather than QRS
is mechanistically plausible, but it is still an internal three-epoch result.
The branch costs approximately 4.16 GB additional peak GPU memory.

The SSL increment is heterogeneous. On lead I it changes mean Pearson by
−0.0005, lower-tail Pearson by −0.0041, and boundary F1 by −0.0011. On lead II
it changes those endpoints by +0.0030, +0.0040, and +0.0358. It also adds about
8.45 GB beyond the wavelet-only model. The correct screen-level conclusion is
therefore **possible lead-II delineation complementarity**, not a general SSL
improvement.

Replacing Morlet phase with UEG phase changes mean Pearson by less than 0.0002
on either lead. Boundary F1 increases by 0.0030 on lead I and 0.0105 on lead II,
but the lower-tail Pearson direction reverses between leads. This is too small
and inconsistent for a physiology claim without longer matched training.

Only the lead-II E1 complementarity cell completed. It is essentially tied with
A0+wavelet+SSL while using approximately 5.94 GB more memory. Because lead I is
missing, this contrast is both single-seed and asymmetrically censored.

Mean Pearson and reconstruction loss are almost redundant in rank
(absolute Spearman approximately 0.99), while boundary F1 correlates only about
0.11 with mean Pearson. Wave mIoU is moderately coupled to both. This explains
why a reconstruction leader need not be a boundary leader and why a single
composite score embeds a scientific value judgement.

With 115 completed rows and many endpoints, the metric-wise maximum is strongly
subject to winner's curse. A configuration appearing once at the top is a
candidate, not a confirmed effect. The registered contrasts above are more
interpretable than post-hoc leaderboard mining, even though they still lack
replication.
