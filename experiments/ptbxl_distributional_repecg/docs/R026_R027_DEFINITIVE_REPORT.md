## 1. Architectural Decision & Executive Summary

### Final Model Freeze: Operator-Span SetOperator
Following a comprehensive audit of prediction continuity across continuous subspace trajectories and baseline models, we make a definitive architectural freeze:

$$\boxed{\textbf{Final Model: Operator-Span SetOperator}}$$
$$\begin{aligned}
y_q(t) &= q^\top x(t) \\
y_q &\longrightarrow [y_q, \dot{y}_q] \longrightarrow \text{Phase-KME Response Atom } r_q \\
(q, r_q) &\longrightarrow \text{SetTransformer Encoders \& Cross-Attention Head}
\end{aligned}$$
with the original SetOperator encoder architecture, trained over the continuous linear measurement functional distribution $q \sim \pi_{\rm train}$.

- **No Canonical-Frame (CF) Front-End:** Rejected as the final architecture due to severe prediction step inflation ($J_{\text{pred}} \approx 11.2\times - 22.6\times$) and unprovoked superclass decision flips in $8.1\% - 14.9\%$ of patients at coordinate pivot boundaries (confirming Dym, Lawrence & Siegel, ICML 2024).
- **No Projected Parseval Front-End:** Relegated to diagnostic status. While smooth, unweighted SetTransformer token multiplicity and frame weight discard ($w_i = P_{ii}$) introduce mathematical inconsistencies without delivering empirical advantage ($\Delta = -0.0014$).
- **No Extra Covariance, Graph, or Auxiliary Objectives:** The architecture remains strictly parameter-efficient ($128$-dim atoms, $5$-class linear readout).

### Exact Marginal Training Law: $\pi_{\rm train}$
During training, each patient record provides 16 precomputed candidate operators: 8 canonical basis leads and 8 continuous linear combinations. Context sets of size $m \sim \text{Uniform}\{1, \ldots, 7\}$ are sampled uniformly at random without replacement from these 16 candidates.

Consequently, the model's exact marginal training-operator exposure distribution is:
$$\boxed{ \pi_{\rm train} = \frac{1}{2} \pi_{\rm basis} + \frac{1}{2} \pi_{\rm aug} = \frac{1}{2} \pi_{\rm basis} + \frac{1}{4} \pi_{\rm dense}^{\rm excl} + \frac{1}{4} \pi_{\rm sparse}^{\rm excl} }$$
where:
1. **Canonical basis anchor ($\pi_{\rm basis}$):**
   $$\pi_{\rm basis} = \frac{1}{8} \sum_{i=1}^8 \delta_{e_i}$$
2. **Dense continuous augmentations ($\pi_{\rm dense}^{\rm excl}$):** Generated as $g \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_8)$, $q = g / \|g\|_2 \sim \text{Haar}(\mathcal{S}^7)$.
3. **Sparse coordinate augmentations ($\pi_{\rm sparse}^{\rm excl}$):** Generated by drawing $k \sim \text{Uniform}(\{2, 3\})$, choosing a coordinate support subset $J \subset \{1, \dots, 8\}$ with $|J| = k$ uniformly at random, sampling $g_J \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_k)$, setting $g_{J^c} = 0$, and normalizing $q = g / \|g\|_2$. The sparse support is the explicit union of coordinate subspheres:
$$\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\rm sparse}^{\rm excl}) = \bigcup_{|J| \in \{2, 3\}} \mathcal{S}^{|J|-1} \subset \mathcal{S}^7.$$
4. **Antipodal exclusion boundary:** Both continuous components reject candidates satisfying $\min_{\sigma \in \{-1, +1\}} \|q - \sigma h\|_2 < 0.08$ for any $h \in \mathcal{H}_{\rm heldout}$, strictly preventing training leakage of the 8 canonical evaluation vectors:
$$\mathcal{H}_{\rm heldout} = \{\text{Lead III}, \text{aVR}, \text{aVL}, \text{aVF}, (V_3 - V_2), (V_1 - V_6), (I + V_4), (II - V_5)\}.$$

This methodological contract confirms that training does not discard standard clinical leads; it **anchors representations on canonical clinical measurements while augmenting them with continuous linear functionals**.

### Prior Art & CVPR Novelty Hypothesis
Prior literature has explored variable-lead ECG representations via discrete lead masking:
- Oh et al. (PMLR 2022) used random lead masking to induce robustness across standard lead subsets.
- K-MERL uses dynamic lead masking for arbitrary named lead configurations.
- LAEF (2026) incorporates stochastic lead sampling in variable-size graph models.
- Outside ECG, operator learning work has applied Set Transformers to variable discrete sensor locations.

Our defensible **novelty hypothesis** for CVPR is strictly distinct:
$$\boxed{\textbf{Training over continuously parameterized linear measurement functionals } y_q(t) = q^\top x(t),}$$
where waveform augmentation is exact linear recombination within the adopted 8-dimensional independent-lead coordinate convention, and the network explicitly consumes the continuous functional $q \in \mathbb{R}^8$ alongside the functional response atom $r_q$.

### The Core Scientific Finding: The Occam Principle
The overarching experimental trajectory reveals a fundamental scientific insight:
$$\boxed{\textbf{For SetOperator, broad support over the measurement-operator distribution matters more than additional architectural machinery.}}$$

Earlier architectural modifications failed to provide robust generalization:
- **R023 $q$-Encoder Modification:** Redundant operator embedding failed to generalize.
- **R025 Observable-Subspace Encoder:** Adding subspace projection tokens added complexity without lifting unseen oblique performance.
- **Hard Canonical Frame (CF):** Produced mathematically exact algebraic quotient invariance but caused unacceptable clinical prediction discontinuities at pivot boundaries (confirming Dym, Lawrence & Siegel, ICML 2024).
- **Operator-Span Training:** In contrast, simple continuous measurement-domain training support yielded a massive, robust $+0.170$ AUROC gain ($0.6842 \to 0.8541$) across 100 held-out Grassmann subspaces and $+0.079$ ($0.7479 \to 0.8265$) on clinical obliques, with zero additional model parameters.

## 3. Analysis: The Pitfalls of Hard Canonicalization on Continuous Trajectories

### Theoretical Context (Dym, Lawrence & Siegel, ICML 2024)
Recent theoretical work in invariant machine learning (Dym, Lawrence & Siegel, ICML 2024; Puny et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2023) has proven that selecting a single canonical representative via hard canonicalization functions generally **destroys topological continuity**: an otherwise smooth target function becomes discontinuous across coordinate boundaries where the canonical representative abruptly switches.

### Empirical Demonstration on Higher-Rank Geodesics
To test whether this mathematical limitation impacts clinical ECG predictions, we audited 15 higher-rank geodesics on $\operatorname{Gr}(r, 8)$ for ranks $r \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ across $N=50$ patients ($1,500$ evaluated states).

For each step along the geodesic $P(t) = \exp(t A) P_0 \exp(-t A)$, we evaluated:
1. **Prediction Step Ratio:** $J_{\text{pred}} = \frac{\|\hat{y}(t_{\text{pivot}}) - \hat{y}(t_{\text{pivot}}-\delta)\|_2}{\operatorname{median}_{t} \|\hat{y}(t+\delta) - \hat{y}(t)\|_2}$.
2. **Clinical Decision Flip Rate:** The fraction of patients whose predicted argmax clinical superclass flips across a single step: $\mathbb{I}[\operatorname{argmax} \hat{y}(t) \ne \operatorname{argmax} \hat{y}(t-\delta)]$.

```
Pivot Boundary Stability (Rank-2 Geodesic):
Canonical Frame:   ═════════════════════════════════════[ J_pred = 22.64x | 14.9% flips ]══════
Parseval Frame:    ───[ J_pred = 1.01x | 1.1% flips ]─────────────────────────────────────────
Operator-Span:     ───[ J_pred = 1.00x | 1.0% flips ]─────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Empirical Audit Results:
| Architecture | Subspace Rank | Pivot Step Ratio ($J_{\text{pred}}$) | Boundary Decision Flip Rate | Interior Decision Flip Rate |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **CF-Span (Hard Section)** | $r=2$ | **$22.64\times$** | **$14.9\%$** | $1.2\%$ |
| **CF-Span (Hard Section)** | $r=3$ | **$11.23\times$** | **$8.1\%$** | $1.0\%$ |
| **CF-Span (Hard Section)** | $r=4$ | **$11.65\times$** | **$9.3\%$** | $1.1\%$ |
| **Projected Parseval Frame** | $r=2$ | $1.01\times$ | $1.1\%$ | $1.1\%$ |
| **Projected Parseval Frame** | $r=3$ | $0.99\times$ | $1.0\%$ | $1.0\%$ |
| **Projected Parseval Frame** | $r=4$ | $1.01\times$ | $1.2\%$ | $1.2\%$ |
| **Operator-Span (Uncanonicalized)** | $r=2$ | $1.00\times$ | $1.0\%$ | $1.0\%$ |

**Conclusion:**  
The nominal $+0.0020$ aggregate AUROC improvement of CF over Span-Only across 13 static configurations cannot justify retaining an architecture that inflates prediction steps by $11\times - 23\times$ and flips diagnoses in $14.9\%$ of patients. Hard canonicalization is scientifically valuable as an analysis experiment illustrating the limits of hard coordinate sections, but must be discarded as the final clinical architecture.

## 4. Mathematical Audit of the Projected Parseval Frame

The Projected Parseval Frame represents an observable subspace $P = Q^+ Q$ through ambient coordinate projections:
$$v_i = P e_i, \qquad P = \sum_{i=1}^8 v_i v_i^\top.$$
Writing $w_i = \|v_i\|^2 = P_{ii}$ and normalizing $u_i = v_i / \sqrt{w_i}$ yields:
$$P = \sum_{i=1}^8 w_i u_i u_i^\top.$$

### Critical Mathematical Inconsistency: Discard of Frame Weights $w_i$
In our implementation audit of the Parseval front-end:
1. The network was supplied with normalized direction vectors $u_i$ and corresponding normalized response waveforms $r(u_i)$.
2. The diagonal frame weights $w_i = P_{ii}$ were **discarded** before feeding the tokens into the SetTransformer.
3. Therefore, the network does not consume the weighted Parseval measure $\mu_P = \sum_i w_i \delta_{u_i}$, but rather an unweighted set $\{u_i\}_{i=1}^8$.

### The Token Multiplicity Pathology
For a rank-1 subspace $P = q q^\top$, ambient projections yield:
$$v_i = q q_i \implies u_i = \operatorname{sign}(q_i) q \quad (\text{whenever } q_i \ne 0).$$
Consequently, a single 1D measurement subspace produces up to eight duplicate copies of $q$ and $-q$. While the SetTransformer is permutation invariant, it is **not multiplicity invariant**: eight identical tokens generate different cross-attention aggregations than a single token.

### Decisive Parseval Non-Inferiority Gate:
To evaluate whether Parseval warrants inclusion as a fallback, we executed a pre-declared non-inferiority gate across all 13 clinical configurations and the 100 held-out Grassmann subspaces:
- Pre-declared non-inferiority margin: $\Delta \ge -0.010$
- Prediction stability requirement: $J_{\text{pred}} \le 1.50$

**Gate Evaluation Results (`r027_parseval_decisive_gate.json`):**
- Full 13-Configuration Mean AUROC: Operator-Span $= 0.8635$, Parseval-Span $= 0.8621$ ($\Delta = \mathbf{-0.0014}$, passes margin).
- 100 Grassmann Subspaces Mean AUROC: Operator-Span $= 0.8541$, Parseval-Span $= 0.8529$ ($\Delta = \mathbf{-0.0012}$, passes margin).
- Trajectory Stability: $J_{\text{pred}} = 1.01 \le 1.50$ (passes stability).
- Exact Quotient Invariance: Achieved by construction ($V(U) \equiv 0$).
- Frame Weight Preservation: **FAILED** (discards $w_i = P_{ii}$, unweighted SetTransformer token multiplicity).
- Incremental Value: **ZERO practical gain** over pure Operator-Span SetOperator.

**Final Verdict:** Relegate Projected Parseval to the paper appendix as an analytical diagnostic showing how smooth frames eliminate pivot boundary discontinuities, but do **not** adopt it as the final model.

### Question 2: External Clinical Cohort Transfer Strategy & Evidence Hierarchy
Following rigorous review, previously drafted preliminary Basis-vs-Span summary arrows have been removed because they lack an end-to-end reproducible inference pipeline. External clinical datasets record **named hardware leads**, not continuously calibrated spatial angles $q \in \mathbb{R}^8$. Without an anatomical subject-specific dipole lead-field matrix $\text{channel} \mapsto q$, external evaluations test **cross-dataset domain transfer under sparse lead subsets**, NOT continuous sensor geometry generalization.

We organize external evaluation into a principled **three-level evaluation tier**:
- **Core (PTB-XL):** Training domain + exact continuous $q$ + 13 clinical configurations + 100 frozen Grassmann subspaces (causal/mechanistic proof of held-out measurement-subspace generalization: $+0.170$ AUROC across ranks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6).
- **Core External (Adversarial & Spatial Stress Tests):**
  - **LUDB:** External 12-lead pathology benchmark; serving as an adversarial test where GraphECG was previously strong.
  - **Zhejiang:** RVOT/LVOT ventricular arrhythmia localization, which is highly spatial and lead-sensitive.
  - **Kingston-ICU:** Sparse ICU rhythm domain.
- **Secondary External:** RDB (independent rhythm/morphology) and ISP (systemic/sex phenotypes).
- **Exploratory / Appendix:** VitalDB (named II/V5 surgical monitoring; not continuously calibrated $q$) and MIMIC-III-Ext-PPG (flagging CSFM pretraining domain overlap).

**Standardized Evaluation Protocol:**  
All encoders ($E_{\text{Basis}}, E_{\text{Span}}, E_{\text{GraphECG}}, E_{\text{CSFM}}$) remain frozen. Downstream linear heads $\hat{y} = W z + b$ are trained on each target cohort. We report:
1. **Primary Transfer Estimand:** $\Delta_{\rm span}(D, S) = \text{AUROC}_{\rm Span}(D, S) - \text{AUROC}_{\rm Basis}(D, S)$.
2. **Configuration Retention:** $R(D, S) = \frac{\text{AUROC}(D, S)}{\text{AUROC}(D, Q_{\rm full})}$.

## 9. Conclusion & Publication Positioning

1. **The Central Conclusion:** For SetOperator, broad support over the measurement-operator distribution matters far more than additional architectural machinery. The frozen final model is the simple, uncanonicalized **Operator-Span SetOperator** ($y_q = q^\top x \to \text{Phase-KME} \to \text{SetTransformer}$), trained over $\pi_{\text{span}}$.
2. **Defensible CVPR Novelty:** Continuous linear measurement-operator learning with physically exact projection $y_q(t) = q^\top x(t)$ where the network consumes the measurement functional $q$ directly alongside the functional response atom $r_q$. This is fundamentally distinct from discrete lead masking or selection.
3. **Topological Discontinuity Analysis:** Hard canonical frames demonstrate empirically why naive coordinate canonicalization fails on continuous trajectories (citing Dym et al., ICML 2024), creating $11\times-23\times$ prediction step inflation and $8.1\%-14.9\%$ decision flips across coordinate pivot boundaries.
4. **Grassmann-100 Benchmark:** Clean, unconfounded evidence of held-out patient + held-out measurement-subspace generalization across ranks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 within PTB-XL-derived physical signals ($+0.170$ AUROC improvement across 100 frozen subspaces).
5. **Calibrated Scope:** External datasets (Kingston, VitalDB, MIMIC) provide exploratory evidence of cross-dataset transfer under named lead subsets; claims of arbitrary real-device continuous geometry generalization are avoided without explicit physical sensor calibration.
6. **Baseline Interface Comparisons:** SetOperator establishes descriptive parity on oblique subcutaneous monitoring ($V_3 - V_2$) with GraphECG while substantially outperforming it on standard sparse and precordial subsets.
