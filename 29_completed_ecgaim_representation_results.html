def compact_ci(frame, digits=3):
    out = frame.copy()
    out["estimate (95% CI)"] = out.apply(
        lambda r: f"{r.value:.{digits}f} [{r.ci_low:.{digits}f}, {r.ci_high:.{digits}f}]"
        if pd.notna(r.ci_low) and pd.notna(r.ci_high) else f"{r.value:.{digits}f}", axis=1)
    return out

9.1 What question this study can answer

This is a frozen-representation audit, not another reconstruction leaderboard. It asks whether a frozen ECG-AIM encoder contains linearly accessible rhythm-code or annotation-timing information; whether that information adds to deliberately small waveform controls; whether 1011011 improves on the raw 1000000 anchor on the same records; and whether latent outliers are associated with reconstruction failure.

The rhythm endpoint is released cross-sectional code membership. The timing endpoint is the annotation interval from QRS onset to T offset; it is explicitly not clinical QT or QTc. Neither endpoint establishes diagnosis, prospective AF prediction, causal physiology, or identifiability of a missing 12-lead ECG.

ImportantBottom line

The latents contain useful rhythm-code information, but the current experiment does not show that 1011011 is a better AF representation than 1000000. 1011011 improves several measures of annotation-interval encoding, while direct waveform summaries remain substantially better for absolute timing. Rhythm codes are locally enriched in latent space but not globally separated.

All 11 extraction jobs are complete. Only the two anchors have train, validation, and test activations. Their probes were selected on validation data and evaluated once on shared test records. The other nine checkpoints are test-only and contribute geometry, similarity, and visualization—not fitted probes. Fitting probes on them would leak the evaluation split.

The pipeline standardizes and fits PCA on training data, restricts probes to at most eight PCA components, applies a validation one-standard-error selection rule favouring simpler candidates, and refits on train plus validation. Test intervals use paired record-level bootstrap resamples. These safeguards do not remove single-split or single-seed uncertainty.

## What question this study can answer

This is a **frozen-representation audit**, not another reconstruction
leaderboard. It asks whether a frozen ECG-AIM encoder contains linearly
accessible rhythm-code or annotation-timing information; whether that
information adds to deliberately small waveform controls; whether `1011011`
improves on the raw `1000000` anchor on the same records; and whether latent
outliers are associated with reconstruction failure.

The rhythm endpoint is released cross-sectional code membership. The timing
endpoint is the annotation interval from QRS onset to T offset; it is explicitly
**not clinical QT or QTc**. Neither endpoint establishes diagnosis, prospective
AF prediction, causal physiology, or identifiability of a missing 12-lead ECG.

::: {.callout-important}
## Bottom line

The latents contain useful rhythm-code information, but the current experiment
does not show that `1011011` is a better AF representation than `1000000`.
`1011011` improves several measures of annotation-interval encoding, while
direct waveform summaries remain substantially better for absolute timing.
Rhythm codes are locally enriched in latent space but not globally separated.
:::
