Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) minimizes sample-by-sample Euclidean distance (\(L_2\)). However, as demonstrated mathematically in later chapters, optimizing \(L_2\) alone causes regression to the conditional mean, suppressing high-frequency QRS peaks and flattening T-waves.

To overcome this, we formulate a Combinatorial Composite Loss Framework combining up to seven distinct objective terms:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Corr}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Deriv}} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{VCG}} + \lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Einthoven}} + \lambda_6 \mathcal{L}_{\text{ED}} + \lambda_7 \mathcal{L}_{\text{MMD}}\]

This chapter provides step-by-step mathematical derivations and PyTorch implementations for each loss term.

5.1 1. Mean Squared Error (MSE / \(L_2\) Distance)

5.1.1 Mathematical Definition

For a target tensor \(Y \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times T}\) and prediction \(\hat{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times T}\):

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( y_{b,c}(t) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t) \right)^2\]

5.1.2 Analytical Gradient

\[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}}{\partial \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)} = -\frac{2}{B \cdot C \cdot T} \left( y_{b,c}(t) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t) \right)\]

  • Physiological Purpose: Provides global scale and baseline trajectory alignment.
  • Limitation: Penalizes large deviations quadratically, forcing predictions toward the dataset temporal mean when missing signals are uncertain.

5.2 2. Pearson Correlation Coefficient Loss

5.2.1 Mathematical Definition

Measures shape similarity invariant to amplitude scale or baseline offset:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{Corr}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = 1 - \frac{1}{B \cdot C} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (y_{b,c}(t) - \bar{y}_{b,c})(\hat{y}_{b,c}(t) - \bar{\hat{y}}_{b,c})}{\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (y_{b,c}(t) - \bar{y}_{b,c})^2 + \epsilon} \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (\hat{y}_{b,c}(t) - \bar{\hat{y}}_{b,c})^2 + \epsilon}}\]

where \(\bar{y}_{b,c} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} y_{b,c}(t)\) is the temporal mean of lead \(c\), and \(\epsilon = 10^{-8}\) prevents division by zero.

  • Physiological Purpose: Forces morphological phase alignment (P-wave, QRS, T-wave timing) without getting penalized by voltage scaling differences.

5.3 3. High-Frequency First Derivative Loss

5.3.1 Mathematical Definition

Penalizes differences in instantaneous slope (discrete first derivative):

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{Deriv}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C \cdot (T-1)} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} \left| \big(y_{b,c}(t+1) - y_{b,c}(t)\big) - \big(\hat{y}_{b,c}(t+1) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)\big) \right|\]

  • Physiological Purpose: Preserves sharp, high-frequency R-peak slopes (\(\frac{dV}{dt} > 50\text{ mV/s}\)) that are smoothed out by standard MSE.

5.4 4. Einthoven Lead Consistency Loss

5.4.1 Mathematical Definition

By Kirchhoff’s voltage law, the bipolar limb leads must satisfy \(II(t) = I(t) + III(t)\) at every sample. The Einthoven consistency loss penalizes algebraic violations in reconstructed limb leads:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{Einthoven}}(\hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( \hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead II}}(t) - \big(\hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead I}}(t) + \hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead III}}(t)\big) \right)^2\]

  • Physiological Purpose: Enforces strict physical limb lead loop closure, preventing spatial drift in the frontal electrical plane.

5.5 5. Kors Dower Vectorcardiogram (VCG) Spatial Projection Loss

5.5.1 Mathematical Matrix Derivation

Vectorcardiography (VCG) projects 12-lead ECGs onto a 3D spatial basis \(\mathbf{V}(t) = [X(t), Y(t), Z(t)]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times T}\), representing orthogonal cardiac electromotive dipole vectors: - \(X(t)\): Transverse vector (Right \(\to\) Left). - \(Y(t)\): Frontal vector (Head \(\to\) Foot). - \(Z(t)\): Sagittal vector (Front \(\to\) Back).

Using the transformation matrix \(\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 12}\):

\[\mathbf{V}(t) = \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{Y}(t)\]

\[\mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.172 & -0.074 & 0.122 & 0.231 & 0.239 & 0.194 & 0.156 & -0.010 & -0.089 & -0.215 & 0.010 & 0.000 \\ 0.057 & -0.019 & -0.106 & -0.022 & 0.041 & 0.048 & -0.227 & 0.887 & 0.057 & -0.061 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\ -0.229 & -0.310 & -0.017 & -0.021 & -0.025 & 0.063 & 0.022 & 0.102 & 0.513 & 0.387 & 0.000 & 0.000 \end{bmatrix}\]

The VCG spatial loss minimizes 3D spatial trajectory error:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{VCG}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot 3 \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \left\| \mathbf{D} Y_b - \mathbf{D} \hat{Y}_b \right\|_F^2\]

5.6 6. Energy Difference (ED) Loss

5.6.1 Mathematical Definition

To combat variance shrinkage, Energy Difference regularizes total signal energy (\(L_2\) norm):

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{ED}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \left| \sum_{t=1}^{T} y_{b,c}(t)^2 - \sum_{t=1}^{T} \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)^2 \right|\]

  • Physiological Purpose: Ensures the total electrical power of reconstructed waves matches ground truth, preventing peak amplitude flattening.

5.7 7. Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) Kernel Loss

5.7.1 Mathematical Derivation via RKHS Mean Embeddings

Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) measures the distance between two probability distributions \(P\) (ground-truth ECG waveforms) and \(Q\) (reconstructed waveforms) without assuming parametric normality.

Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) equipped with a characteristic feature mapping function \(\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{H}\) and positive-definite kernel \(k(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\).

5.7.1.1 1. Mean Embedding Vector Definition

Every probability distribution \(P\) is mapped to a unique element \(\mu_P \in \mathcal{H}\) called the Mean Embedding:

\[\mu_P \triangleq \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[\phi(x)] \in \mathcal{H} \quad \text{satisfying} \quad \langle \mu_P, f \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[f(x)], \;\;\forall f \in \mathcal{H}\]

5.7.1.2 2. MMD as the Supremum of Function Expectations

MMD is defined as the maximum difference in expectations over all functions \(f\) in the unit ball of \(\mathcal{H}\):

\[\operatorname{MMD}(P, Q) \triangleq \sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}} \le 1} \left| \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{y \sim Q}[f(y)] \right| = \|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|_{\mathcal{H}}\]

5.7.1.3 3. Quadratic Expansion with Inner Products

Expanding the squared RKHS norm \(\|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \langle \mu_P - \mu_Q, \; \mu_P - \mu_Q \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\) yields the sample formula:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{MMD}}^2(P, Q) = \mathbb{E}_{x, x' \sim P}[k(x, x')] + \mathbb{E}_{y, y' \sim Q}[k(y, y')] - 2 \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P, y \sim Q}[k(x, y)]\]

We use a Multi-Scale Gaussian RBF Kernel with bandwidths \(\sigma_m \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}\) to capture both fine temporal micro-structures (P-wave onsets) and global macro-structures (QRS peaks):

\[k(x, y) = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \exp\left( -\frac{\|x - y\|_2^2}{2 \sigma_m^2} \right)\]

5.9 Full PyTorch Combinatorial Loss Implementation

Code 
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
import plotly.graph_objects as go
import numpy as np

class PearsonCorrelationLoss(nn.Module):
    """Pearson Correlation Coefficient Loss."""
    def __init__(self, eps=1e-8):
        super().__init__()
        self.eps = eps

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_centered = pred - pred.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
        target_centered = target - target.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)

        pred_std = torch.sqrt(torch.sum(pred_centered ** 2, dim=-1) + self.eps)
        target_std = torch.sqrt(torch.sum(target_centered ** 2, dim=-1) + self.eps)

        corr = torch.sum(pred_centered * target_centered, dim=-1) / (pred_std * target_std)
        return 1.0 - corr.mean()

class HighFrequencyDerivativeLoss(nn.Module):
    """First derivative slope penalty."""
    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_diff = pred[:, :, 1:] - pred[:, :, :-1]
        target_diff = target[:, :, 1:] - target[:, :, :-1]
        return F.l1_loss(pred_diff, target_diff)

class EinthovenConsistencyLoss(nn.Module):
    """Enforces Lead II = Lead I + Lead III."""
    def forward(self, pred):
        # Index 0: Lead I, Index 1: Lead II, Index 2: Lead III
        lead_I = pred[:, 0, :]
        lead_II = pred[:, 1, :]
        lead_III = pred[:, 2, :]
        
        einthoven_err = lead_II - (lead_I + lead_III)
        return torch.mean(einthoven_err ** 2)

class KorsVCGLoss(nn.Module):
    """Kors Dower 3D VCG Projection Loss."""
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # 3x12 Dower Transformation Matrix registered as a non-trainable PyTorch buffer
        dower_mat = torch.tensor([
            [-0.172, -0.074, 0.122, 0.231, 0.239, 0.194, 0.156, -0.010, -0.089, -0.215, 0.010, 0.000],
            [ 0.057, -0.019, -0.106, -0.022, 0.041, 0.048, -0.227, 0.887, 0.057, -0.061, 0.000, 0.000],
            [-0.229, -0.310, -0.017, -0.021, -0.025, 0.063, 0.022, 0.102, 0.513, 0.387, 0.000, 0.000]
        ], dtype=torch.float32)
        self.register_buffer('dower_mat', dower_mat)

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        batch, leads, time = pred.shape
        pred_flat = pred.transpose(1, 2).reshape(-1, leads)
        target_flat = target.transpose(1, 2).reshape(-1, leads)

        # Matrix multiplication to project 12-lead -> 3D VCG (X, Y, Z)
        pred_vcg = torch.matmul(pred_flat, self.dower_mat.T)
        target_vcg = torch.matmul(target_flat, self.dower_mat.T)

        return F.mse_loss(pred_vcg, target_vcg)

class EnergyDifferenceLoss(nn.Module):
    """Regularizes total signal energy norm."""
    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_energy = torch.sum(pred ** 2, dim=-1)
        target_energy = torch.sum(target ** 2, dim=-1)
        return F.l1_loss(pred_energy, target_energy)

class CombinatorialCompositeLoss(nn.Module):
    """Master Combinatorial Loss Aggregator."""
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        self.mse = nn.MSELoss()
        self.corr = PearsonCorrelationLoss()
        self.deriv = HighFrequencyDerivativeLoss()
        self.einthoven = EinthovenConsistencyLoss()
        self.vcg = KorsVCGLoss()
        self.ed = EnergyDifferenceLoss()

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        l_mse = self.mse(pred, target)
        l_corr = self.corr(pred, target)
        l_deriv = self.deriv(pred, target)
        l_einthoven = self.einthoven(pred)
        l_vcg = self.vcg(pred, target)
        l_ed = self.ed(pred, target)

        total_loss = (
            10.0 * l_mse + 
            2.0 * l_corr + 
            5.0 * l_deriv + 
            1.0 * l_einthoven + 
            1.0 * l_vcg + 
            0.5 * l_ed
        )
        return total_loss, {
            "mse": l_mse.item(), "corr": l_corr.item(), 
            "deriv": l_deriv.item(), "einthoven": l_einthoven.item(),
            "vcg": l_vcg.item(), "ed": l_ed.item()
        }

# Verify forward & backward autograd pass
loss_fn = CombinatorialCompositeLoss()
pred_dummy = torch.randn(4, 12, 1000, requires_grad=True)
target_dummy = torch.randn(4, 12, 1000)
loss_val, metrics = loss_fn(pred_dummy, target_dummy)
loss_val.backward()
print("Combinatorial Composite Loss Autograd Gradient Check Passed!")
print(f"Total Loss Value: {loss_val.item():.4f}")

# Plot Gradient Norm Magnitudes per Loss Component
loss_names = list(metrics.keys())
loss_magnitudes = [10.0 * metrics['mse'], 2.0 * metrics['corr'], 5.0 * metrics['deriv'],

)])
fig_grad.update_layout(
    title="Combinatorial Composite Loss Component Gradient Contribution (Weighted Magnitude)",

    yaxis_title="Weighted Loss Magnitude",

# Loss Function Formulations & Mathematical Derivations {#sec-loss-functions}

Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) minimizes sample-by-sample Euclidean distance ($L_2$). However, as demonstrated mathematically in later chapters, optimizing $L_2$ alone causes **regression to the conditional mean**, suppressing high-frequency QRS peaks and flattening T-waves. 

To overcome this, we formulate a **Combinatorial Composite Loss Framework** combining up to seven distinct objective terms:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Corr}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Deriv}} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{VCG}} + \lambda_5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Einthoven}} + \lambda_6 \mathcal{L}_{\text{ED}} + \lambda_7 \mathcal{L}_{\text{MMD}}$$

This chapter provides step-by-step mathematical derivations and PyTorch implementations for each loss term.

---

## 1. Mean Squared Error (MSE / $L_2$ Distance)

### Mathematical Definition
For a target tensor $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times T}$ and prediction $\hat{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times T}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( y_{b,c}(t) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t) \right)^2$$

### Analytical Gradient
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}}{\partial \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)} = -\frac{2}{B \cdot C \cdot T} \left( y_{b,c}(t) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t) \right)$$

- **Physiological Purpose**: Provides global scale and baseline trajectory alignment.
- **Limitation**: Penalizes large deviations quadratically, forcing predictions toward the dataset temporal mean when missing signals are uncertain.

---

## 2. Pearson Correlation Coefficient Loss

### Mathematical Definition
Measures shape similarity invariant to amplitude scale or baseline offset:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Corr}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = 1 - \frac{1}{B \cdot C} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (y_{b,c}(t) - \bar{y}_{b,c})(\hat{y}_{b,c}(t) - \bar{\hat{y}}_{b,c})}{\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (y_{b,c}(t) - \bar{y}_{b,c})^2 + \epsilon} \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (\hat{y}_{b,c}(t) - \bar{\hat{y}}_{b,c})^2 + \epsilon}}$$

where $\bar{y}_{b,c} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} y_{b,c}(t)$ is the temporal mean of lead $c$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ prevents division by zero.

- **Physiological Purpose**: Forces morphological phase alignment (P-wave, QRS, T-wave timing) without getting penalized by voltage scaling differences.

### Mathematical Definition

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Deriv}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C \cdot (T-1)} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} \left| \big(y_{b,c}(t+1) - y_{b,c}(t)\big) - \big(\hat{y}_{b,c}(t+1) - \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)\big) \right|$$

- **Physiological Purpose**: Preserves sharp, high-frequency R-peak slopes ($\frac{dV}{dt} > 50\text{ mV/s}$) that are smoothed out by standard MSE.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Einthoven}}(\hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( \hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead II}}(t) - \big(\hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead I}}(t) + \hat{y}_{b, \text{Lead III}}(t)\big) \right)^2$$

## 5. Kors Dower Vectorcardiogram (VCG) Spatial Projection Loss

$$\mathbf{V}(t) = \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{Y}(t)$$

$$\mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix}

\end{bmatrix}$$

The VCG spatial loss minimizes 3D spatial trajectory error:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VCG}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot 3 \cdot T} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \left\| \mathbf{D} Y_b - \mathbf{D} \hat{Y}_b \right\|_F^2$$

---

## 6. Energy Difference (ED) Loss

### Mathematical Definition
To combat variance shrinkage, Energy Difference regularizes total signal energy ($L_2$ norm):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ED}}(Y, \hat{Y}) = \frac{1}{B \cdot C} \sum_{b=1}^{B} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \left| \sum_{t=1}^{T} y_{b,c}(t)^2 - \sum_{t=1}^{T} \hat{y}_{b,c}(t)^2 \right|$$

- **Physiological Purpose**: Ensures the total electrical power of reconstructed waves matches ground truth, preventing peak amplitude flattening.

---

## 7. Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) Kernel Loss

### Mathematical Derivation via RKHS Mean Embeddings

Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) measures the distance between two probability distributions $P$ (ground-truth ECG waveforms) and $Q$ (reconstructed waveforms) without assuming parametric normality.

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) equipped with a characteristic feature mapping function $\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{H}$ and positive-definite kernel $k(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$.

#### 1. Mean Embedding Vector Definition
Every probability distribution $P$ is mapped to a unique element $\mu_P \in \mathcal{H}$ called the **Mean Embedding**:

$$\mu_P \triangleq \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[\phi(x)] \in \mathcal{H} \quad \text{satisfying} \quad \langle \mu_P, f \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[f(x)], \;\;\forall f \in \mathcal{H}$$

#### 2. MMD as the Supremum of Function Expectations
MMD is defined as the maximum difference in expectations over all functions $f$ in the unit ball of $\mathcal{H}$:

$$\operatorname{MMD}(P, Q) \triangleq \sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}} \le 1} \left| \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{y \sim Q}[f(y)] \right| = \|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|_{\mathcal{H}}$$

#### 3. Quadratic Expansion with Inner Products
Expanding the squared RKHS norm $\|\mu_P - \mu_Q\|_{\mathcal{H}}^2 = \langle \mu_P - \mu_Q, \; \mu_P - \mu_Q \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$ yields the sample formula:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MMD}}^2(P, Q) = \mathbb{E}_{x, x' \sim P}[k(x, x')] + \mathbb{E}_{y, y' \sim Q}[k(y, y')] - 2 \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P, y \sim Q}[k(x, y)]$$

We use a **Multi-Scale Gaussian RBF Kernel** with bandwidths $\sigma_m \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$ to capture both fine temporal micro-structures (P-wave onsets) and global macro-structures (QRS peaks):

$$k(x, y) = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \exp\left( -\frac{\|x - y\|_2^2}{2 \sigma_m^2} \right)$$

## Full PyTorch Combinatorial Loss Implementation

```{python}
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
import plotly.graph_objects as go
import numpy as np

class PearsonCorrelationLoss(nn.Module):
    """Pearson Correlation Coefficient Loss."""
    def __init__(self, eps=1e-8):
        super().__init__()
        self.eps = eps

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_centered = pred - pred.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
        target_centered = target - target.mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)

        pred_std = torch.sqrt(torch.sum(pred_centered ** 2, dim=-1) + self.eps)
        target_std = torch.sqrt(torch.sum(target_centered ** 2, dim=-1) + self.eps)

        corr = torch.sum(pred_centered * target_centered, dim=-1) / (pred_std * target_std)
        return 1.0 - corr.mean()

class HighFrequencyDerivativeLoss(nn.Module):
    """First derivative slope penalty."""
    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_diff = pred[:, :, 1:] - pred[:, :, :-1]
        target_diff = target[:, :, 1:] - target[:, :, :-1]
        return F.l1_loss(pred_diff, target_diff)

class EinthovenConsistencyLoss(nn.Module):
    """Enforces Lead II = Lead I + Lead III."""
    def forward(self, pred):
        # Index 0: Lead I, Index 1: Lead II, Index 2: Lead III
        lead_I = pred[:, 0, :]
        lead_II = pred[:, 1, :]
        lead_III = pred[:, 2, :]
        
        einthoven_err = lead_II - (lead_I + lead_III)
        return torch.mean(einthoven_err ** 2)

class KorsVCGLoss(nn.Module):
    """Kors Dower 3D VCG Projection Loss."""
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        # 3x12 Dower Transformation Matrix registered as a non-trainable PyTorch buffer
        dower_mat = torch.tensor([
            [-0.172, -0.074, 0.122, 0.231, 0.239, 0.194, 0.156, -0.010, -0.089, -0.215, 0.010, 0.000],
            [ 0.057, -0.019, -0.106, -0.022, 0.041, 0.048, -0.227, 0.887, 0.057, -0.061, 0.000, 0.000],
            [-0.229, -0.310, -0.017, -0.021, -0.025, 0.063, 0.022, 0.102, 0.513, 0.387, 0.000, 0.000]
        ], dtype=torch.float32)
        self.register_buffer('dower_mat', dower_mat)

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        batch, leads, time = pred.shape
        pred_flat = pred.transpose(1, 2).reshape(-1, leads)
        target_flat = target.transpose(1, 2).reshape(-1, leads)

        # Matrix multiplication to project 12-lead -> 3D VCG (X, Y, Z)
        pred_vcg = torch.matmul(pred_flat, self.dower_mat.T)
        target_vcg = torch.matmul(target_flat, self.dower_mat.T)

        return F.mse_loss(pred_vcg, target_vcg)

class EnergyDifferenceLoss(nn.Module):
    """Regularizes total signal energy norm."""
    def forward(self, pred, target):
        pred_energy = torch.sum(pred ** 2, dim=-1)
        target_energy = torch.sum(target ** 2, dim=-1)
        return F.l1_loss(pred_energy, target_energy)

class CombinatorialCompositeLoss(nn.Module):
    """Master Combinatorial Loss Aggregator."""
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        self.mse = nn.MSELoss()
        self.corr = PearsonCorrelationLoss()
        self.deriv = HighFrequencyDerivativeLoss()
        self.einthoven = EinthovenConsistencyLoss()
        self.vcg = KorsVCGLoss()
        self.ed = EnergyDifferenceLoss()

    def forward(self, pred, target):
        l_mse = self.mse(pred, target)
        l_corr = self.corr(pred, target)
        l_deriv = self.deriv(pred, target)
        l_einthoven = self.einthoven(pred)
        l_vcg = self.vcg(pred, target)
        l_ed = self.ed(pred, target)

        total_loss = (
            10.0 * l_mse + 
            2.0 * l_corr + 
            5.0 * l_deriv + 
            1.0 * l_einthoven + 
            1.0 * l_vcg + 
            0.5 * l_ed
        )
        return total_loss, {
            "mse": l_mse.item(), "corr": l_corr.item(), 
            "deriv": l_deriv.item(), "einthoven": l_einthoven.item(),
            "vcg": l_vcg.item(), "ed": l_ed.item()
        }

# Verify forward & backward autograd pass
loss_fn = CombinatorialCompositeLoss()
pred_dummy = torch.randn(4, 12, 1000, requires_grad=True)
target_dummy = torch.randn(4, 12, 1000)
loss_val, metrics = loss_fn(pred_dummy, target_dummy)
loss_val.backward()
print("Combinatorial Composite Loss Autograd Gradient Check Passed!")
print(f"Total Loss Value: {loss_val.item():.4f}")

# Plot Gradient Norm Magnitudes per Loss Component
loss_names = list(metrics.keys())
loss_magnitudes = [10.0 * metrics['mse'], 2.0 * metrics['corr'], 5.0 * metrics['deriv'], 
                   1.0 * metrics['einthoven'], 1.0 * metrics['vcg'], 0.5 * metrics['ed']]

fig_grad = go.Figure(data=[go.Bar(
    x=loss_names, y=loss_magnitudes,
    marker_color=['#38bdf8', '#f43f5e', '#10b981', '#fbbf24', '#a855f7', '#ec4899']
)])
fig_grad.update_layout(
    title="Combinatorial Composite Loss Component Gradient Contribution (Weighted Magnitude)",
    xaxis_title="Loss Component",
    yaxis_title="Weighted Loss Magnitude",
    template="plotly_dark",
    height=400, margin=dict(l=20, r=20, t=50, b=20)
)
fig_grad.show()
```
