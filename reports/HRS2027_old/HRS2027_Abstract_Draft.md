**Title:** When Accurate Atrial Fibrillation Burden Is Insufficient: Reliability of Temporal Density Measurements in Ambulatory Electrocardiography  
**Category:** Clinical Electrophysiology — Atrial Fibrillation

Background:
Cumulative atrial fibrillation (AF) burden omits how episodes cluster. Charitos AF density (0=scattered paroxysms to 1=one block) and longest-episode proportion measure clustering, yet their reliability near continuous AF is unclear.

Objective:
To test how high-burden scaling, binary segment calls, R-R irregularity AF detection, and <10-s non-AF labels alter AF density and continuity.

Methods:
In 349 Holter recordings (329 patients, 3 cohorts), we studied 182 multi-hour ECGs (177 patients; 148 mixed, 22 sinus, 12 continuous AF), comparing error-free binary calls (>=50% AF) vs % AF time in 10-s to 60-min segments, plus a single-lead QRS/60-beat R-R irregularity AF algorithm. In 19 >=80%-burden patients (first 12 h), we tested 1,078 <10-s breaks by 2-lead ECG and 2^k assignments.

Results:
Bridging short breaks near 100% burden keeps AF aggregation constant while shrinking (1-burden), scaling density error 1/(1-burden) (500x at 99.8%). Binary segment calls erased short breaks, leaving density undefined in 7.4%–41.9% of 148 mixed ECGs; % AF time kept 100% defined (10-s error 0.00001 vs 0.0104). R-R detection falsely switched rhythms in 23/34 (67.6%) uniform ECGs (16/22 sinus, 7/12 AF) and erred >0.15 in longest-episode proportion in 32.4%–40.6% when burden/density matched <0.01. Masking ventricular arrhythmias and <60-s bradycardia left 16/19 patients (median 11.8 s) with no confirmed non-AF breaks; 1,024 assignments of 10 breaks (21.6 s) spanned density 0.1637–0.9682 or undefined (99.95%–100.00% burden).

Conclusion:
Accurate AF burden does not ensure reliable density or continuity; clustering metrics need % AF time per segment, uncertainty bounds, and <10-s ECG adjudication.
