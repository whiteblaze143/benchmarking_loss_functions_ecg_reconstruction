15.1 A distinct input contract and spatial projection problem

Single-lead ECG reconstruction presents an inherently underdetermined inverse problem: estimating 11 missing lead waveforms from a single observed lead (Lead I or Lead II).

Unlike 3-lead models (which observe Leads I, II, and V_2 simultaneously spanning both frontal and transverse planes), a 1-lead model observes only a single 1D projection of the 3D cardiac electrical dipole vector \mathbf{p}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^3: V_L(t) = \mathbf{c}_L \cdot \mathbf{p}(t) + \eta_L(t) where \mathbf{c}_L \in \mathbb{R}^3 represents the lead vector defined by lead electrode geometry.

15.6 Core mechanism questions and claim hierarchy

  1. A0 \to Spatial Encoding: Does supplying explicit anatomical coordinates (\theta, \phi, z) improve missing-lead estimation over an unconditioned base?
  2. True vs Permuted Geometry (E_1 vs PG_1): Does the network genuinely utilize physiological spatial relationships, or does any arbitrary positional embedding suffice?
  3. Capacity Matching (E_1 vs CM_1): Are observed gains attributable to anatomical conditioning or simply increased parameter capacity?
  4. Generalization & Transport: Do internal validation advantages persist on external cohorts (RDB)?

The empirical resolutions to these questions are detailed in: - Chapter 27: Comprehensive analysis of internal validation results and learning dynamics across all 60 runs. - Chapter 26: External validation on the 360-record Russian Database (RDB) cohort.
