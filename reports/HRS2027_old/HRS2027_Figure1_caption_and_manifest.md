## Extended Figure Legend
**Figure 1. Limits of Temporal Atrial Fibrillation Density Measurement in Ambulatory Electrocardiography.**  
**(A)** Representative unfiltered 2-lead Holter ECG (`128 Hz`, `25.0 mm/s`, `10.0 mm/mV`) showing a `5.16-s` target non-AF break (`[0.00 s, +5.16 s]`) during near-continuous AF; patient identity and native database annotations are withheld pending blinded electrophysiologist adjudication.  
**(B)** Exhaustive `1,024` ($2^{10}$) AF vs. non-AF assignments across `10` brief (`<60 s`, totaling `21.60 s`) breaks in one `12-h` high-burden recording (`99.95%–100.00%` certified AF burden). Across `1,023` mixed-rhythm assignments, Charitos AF density spans `0.1637` to `0.9682` (native baseline `0.1655`), and becomes mathematically undefined if all `10` breaks represent ongoing AF.  
**(C)** Percentage of `n = 148` mixed AF/non-AF multi-hour Holter recordings retaining a mathematically defined Charitos AF density when the recording is divided into consecutive `10-s` to `60-min` segments with zero classification errors: binary yes/no segment calls ($\ge 50\%$ AF) drop measurability from `92.6%` (`137/148`) to `58.1%` (`86/148`), whereas recording the exact percentage of AF time inside each segment preserves `100.0%` (`148/148`) measurability across all segment lengths (`10-s` mean absolute density error `0.00001` vs `0.01038`).
