17.3 Integrated findings by scientific question

17.3.1 1. What is currently the strongest one-lead result?

There is no endpoint-independent winner. At 15 epochs, R5 (Morlet magnitude + UEG Wyatt phase) has the strongest broad lead-II profile: Pearson 0.7607, Pearson p05 0.4304, wave mIoU 0.8627, macro wave F1 0.9261, and boundary F1 0.8886. On lead I, the simpler A0 wavelet/no-SSL model retains the best mean Pearson and reconstruction loss, while R7 leads several boundary and T-wave endpoints. These are internal validation estimates, not external RDB rankings.

Externally, B1 panorama at mask 1010010 gives the best one-lead RDB boundary F1 on both observed leads (0.6585 lead I; 0.6742 lead II). Other spatial models lead waveform lower-tail metrics. Selecting “the best” therefore requires an intended endpoint: waveform similarity, difficult-record protection, event timing, or region overlap.

17.3.2 2. What do wavelets add?

The clean three-epoch A0 contrast shows that a wavelet branch primarily improves P/T delineation: P-wave IoU rises by 0.171 on lead I and 0.083 on lead II, whereas QRS IoU changes by only 0.001–0.003. This is consistent with the longer convergence data, where QRS is near saturation and additional epochs mostly improve P/T and boundaries. The cost is approximately 4.16 GB additional peak GPU memory at screening scale.

This supports a morphology-specific wavelet mechanism, but not yet a general clinical benefit. The wavelet checkpoints have not undergone the same external RDB protocol as the completed legacy spatial models.

17.3.3 3. What does SSL add?

At three epochs, adding SSL to the wavelet branch is neutral/slightly adverse on lead I and favourable for lead-II delineation, while adding about 8.45 GB peak memory. The 15-epoch generation lacks a matched A0+wavelet+SSL cell, so the registered A0 raw → A0+wavelet → A0+wavelet+SSL chain is not complete at the confirmatory horizon. R5 cannot isolate SSL because it changes the physiological view simultaneously. The honest conclusion is possible lead-specific complementarity, not an identified SSL main effect.

17.3.4 4. Does physiological geometry help?

Internally, true E1 geometry beats both permuted geometry and a capacity-matched control in all six mask×lead cells. However, E1 is nearly tied with A0 in five of those six cells. On external RDB, E1-control differences reverse sign across masks, leads, and endpoints. Correct geometry is used by the network, but the data do not support E1 as a generally improved base architecture or a robust external advantage.

17.3.5 5. What do the linear probes establish?

Frozen ECG-AIM latents contain AF/AFIB-code information beyond heart rate, RMS, and spectral entropy. The enhanced anchor’s latent AUROC is 0.787 versus 0.713 for waveform summaries, with calibrated-probability improvements surviving the prespecified correction. Yet 1011011 does not significantly beat 1000000 in direct paired AF metrics: the AUROC difference is +0.027 with a 95% interval from −0.023 to +0.076.

For annotation QRS-onset–to–T-offset duration, 1011011 improves latent-only RMSE and concordance relative to 1000000, but both latents are much worse than direct waveform summaries for absolute MAE. This is representation-content evidence, not diagnostic superiority or a clinical QT/QTc result.

High-dimensional tests find significant local rhythm-code neighbour enrichment but negative, non-significant silhouettes. UMAPs can illustrate local structure but cannot be described as globally separated rhythm manifolds.

17.3.6 6. How well do results transport?

Among the 31 shared three-lead models, LUDB-to-RDB boundary-F1 rank correlation is 0.736. Waveform-tail rank correlations are weak (0.300 for Pearson p05 and approximately zero for MSE p95). Boundary retention is therefore more stable than waveform-fidelity ranking.

For the 60 one-lead spatial models, internal validation correlates only moderately with full RDB endpoints. The 48-record RDB pilot predicts boundary ranking well enough for screening (Spearman 0.82–0.87) but predicts signal-p05 ranking poorly (0.45). Model promotion based on one surrogate will miss endpoint-specific external leaders.

17.3.7 7. Which apparent gains are evaluator effects?

LUDB semi-supervised results show that padding and evaluation-window choice can change foreground mIoU by roughly 0.25—orders of magnitude larger than the student-versus-EMA differences. Two tolerance rows also reuse identical region predictions. The checkpoint, state, split, padding, window, evaluator hash, and tolerance jointly define the estimand; rows differing only in event tolerance must not be counted as independent region evidence.

17.5 Next execution order

  1. Complete or formally freeze the missing MSVAE and ECG-AIM clinical cells.
  2. Extend missing_leads_v2 first to the remaining MSVAE checkpoints, then to ECG-AIM using the same evaluation code and PTB-XL record order.
  3. Create a common per-record seven-mask inference ledger keyed by checkpoint digest, architecture, mask, and seed.
  4. Add the missing matched 15-epoch A0+wavelet+SSL cells, then replicate the A0→wavelet→SSL and Morlet-phase→UEG-phase contrasts with multiple seeds.
  5. Keep the pending 1111002 and 48-cell spatial-v1 protocols separate from completed results until they produce terminal summaries.
  6. Run blinded LUDB/RDB external delineation only for preregistered promoted models, preserving six-boundary RDB validity and avoiding development/test reuse.
  7. Extract all-split embeddings for preregistered one-lead wavelet checkpoints, then repeat the same frozen linear-probe protocol without fitting on test-only checkpoints.
  8. Compare the latent probes with a strong native-waveform classifier and report calibration, decision curves, and external validation before making any clinical-assistance claim.

17.7 Stop rules

  • Do not launch broad external evaluation for incomplete or provenance-failed cells.
  • Do not interpret absence from a database as zero performance.
  • Do not pool validation and test metrics or four-mask and seven-mask studies.
  • Do not promote a one-seed maximum from a 160- or 120-cell screen.
  • Do not compress incompatible clinical endpoints into an unvalidated score.
