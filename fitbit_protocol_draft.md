1.0	BACKGROUND 
Smartwatch devices are increasingly used in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF). These devices potentially allow for a non-invasive means of cardiac monitoring, allowing the detection of AF through various notifications. For example, some commercially available smartwatches now include an irregular rhythm notification (IRR) that detects irregularity in the photoplethysmography signal recorded by the device. Based on the IRR, the smartwatch will notify the user of possible AF.

The IRR notification and related algorithms have been incorporated by Apple into an “AFib History” (AFH) feature included in the Health app. The AFH feature determines the approximate duration the patient is in AF, calculating a percentage that is presented in the Health app. The AFH provides a surrogate measure of AF burden, defined as the percentage of time a person’s heart is in AF during the monitoring period. AF burden is a metric that is increasingly used in the management of AF, and the evaluation of outcomes after a catheter ablation procedure.

Other vendors, such as FitBit, have created an easily accessible Application Programming Interface (API) that allows for easy access to data recorded on the smartwatch or wearable device. This potentially enables clinicians to easily access health and ECG data recorded on the smartwatch, and could facilitate clinical decision-making based on smartwatch-detected health data. Our objective is to evaluate the ECG function of the FitBit Sense as a means of detecting recurrent AF in patients at high risk for AF recurrence.

2.0	RESEARCH QUESTION
What is the yield of using a smartwatch device to detect recurrent AF, as compared to conventional clinical monitoring, in patients at high risk for AF recurrence?

2.1	HYPOTHESIS
We hypothesize that the smartwatch will effectively detect AF recurrence, earlier than detected on conventional clinical monitoring.
 
2.2	INCLUSION CRITERIA
•	All participants must be age ≥18 years old with persistent atrial fibrillation undergoing a planned catheter ablation of AF or cardioversion of AF.
•	Participants must be able to use a smartwatch device (FitBit Sense) and will be able to perform ECG recordings using the smartwatch.

2.3	EXCLUSION CRITERIA
•	Participants who are unable to use the smartwatch device.

2.4	PRIMARY OUTCOME
The primary outcome is a comparison of time to AF detection recording using the smartwatch compared to conventional clinical monitoring.

2.5	SECONDARY OUTCOMES
After a catheter ablation procedure, various cardiac and activity metrics automatically collected by the FitBit Sense will be collected. These metrics include high/low heart rate notifications; resting, walking, and post-workout heart rates; heart rate variability; ECG app recordings; irregular rhythm notifications; cardio fitness level; and cardio recovery level.

3.0	STUDY DESIGN AND METHODS
This is a single-center cohort study conducted at a tertiary care hospital (Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre). Eligible participants will be recruited prospectively from the Arrhythmia Clinic by various cardiac electrophysiologists. Prior to the catheter ablation procedure, participants will be offered the opportunity to participate in the study.

All participants will use the smartwatch device and also receive standard of care clinical monitoring, through the use of an external patch monitor (CardioSTAT™) applied at 3 months and 6 months after the index visit (catheter ablation or cardioversion).

3.1	FITBIT DEVICE
Participants will wear the FitBit Sense device after a catheter ablation or cardioversion procedure for AF. Participants will be asked to use the FitBit Sense ECG function on a daily basis to record their cardiac rhythm for the first 6 months after the catheter ablation or cardioversion procedure, or until recurrent AF is detected, whichever is earlier. 

3.2	STANDARD OF CARE
In addition to using the smartwatch, all participants will also routinely perform standard-of-care monitoring using a clinical-grade cardiac patch monitor at 3 months and 6 months after the index visit (after catheter ablation or cardioversion). This is considered the standard of care monitoring for patients following a catheter ablation or cardioversion procedure.

CardioSTAT™ is a Health Canada-approved, single-lead ambulatory ECG monitor that has been validated for detection and monitoring of AF. Readings are made through 2 electrodes positioned in a lead 1-like configuration, with capacity for 48-hour, 7-day and 14-day recordings. For this study, the devices will be provided to the participant for self-application along with a pre-paid envelope at 3 months and 6 months after the index visit. After completion of monitoring, participants will mail the device for analysis.

All analysis will be performed by iCentia® with de-identified reports provided to the investigators for analysis. iCentia® provides clinical-grade reports. These reports will also be provided to the primary cardiac electrophysiologist performing the ablation procedure.


3.3	DATA COLLECTION AND TRANSFER
Baseline participant demographics including age, sex, AF diagnosis and subtype, medical comorbidities, and medication use will be recorded. Ablation procedure details will be recorded, including ablation targets and any procedural complications. These data collection variables are provided in the Enrollment and Ablation Case Report Forms.

FitBit Sense ECG data will be collected through a custom dashboard that has been developed by the investigator and co-applicants to facilitate the research aims. The dashboard enables team members to download and store ECG data directly onto a Sunnybrook password-protected workstation.

The CardioSTAT™ device recordings and metrics will be returned through the iCentia® secure portal, which is routinely used for transmission of reports. Once received, all identifying data will be reported and replaced with the patient identifier. 

3.4	SAMPLE SIZE AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
The primary analysis will include comparing the primary outcome (time to AF detection) between the smartwatch and conventional clinical monitoring. It is anticipated that smartwatch monitoring using the FitBit Sense may detect a higher rate of recurrent AF, compared to conventional clinical monitoring using the patch monitor. Using estimated AF recurrence rates of 20% after catheter ablation and/or cardioversion with a patch monitor, and a 5% absolute increase in detection of AF recurrence to 25% using the smartwatch, a non-inferiority margin of 10%, alpha of 5%, and power of 80%, we would require approximately 96 patients using both devices to demonstrate non-inferiority of the smartwatch.

As this is a pilot/vanguard study, we will start with a plan to enroll 20 consecutive patients seen in the Arrhythmia Clinic who meet the study inclusion criteria, and plan to expand to the full cohort once feasibility has been demonstrated.

4.0	FUNDING AND ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS
4.1	STUDY FUNDING 
This study is an investigator-initiated study with no external funding. The investigators have access to the FitBit Sense smartwatch devices, which will be loaned to participants and returned at the end of the monitoring period. The patch monitors will be performed at 3 months and 6 months post-index visit and are considered part of the standard of care. 

4.2	CONFIDENTIALITY AND DATA SECURITY
This project will be conducted in compliance with the protocol and principles laid down in the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice (GCP), as defined by the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) where applicable, along with all other local regulatory requirements. Before study initiation, the study will receive approval/favorable opinion from the Sunnybrook Institutional Review Board for the protocol and data collection forms. The principal investigator and co-investigators will have completed Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct for Research Involving Humans (TCPS 2) certificate requirements. 

The data collected will be anonymized by removing patient identifiers, such as name and OHIP number. Enrolled patients will be assigned a unique study identification number. The key code will be stored on an encrypted USB stick. All study data will be stored on another encrypted USB stick. The USB sticks will be stored in separate locked drawers by the Principal Investigator when not in use. 

4.3	ANTICIPATED RISKS
This study does not present any physical risks. The risk of loss of confidentiality is addressed by the data security measures of the study design. 

4.4	CONSENT PROCESS
Eligible patients will be provided information about the study at the Arrhythmia Clinic prior to their ablation. The study personnel will serve as an independent individual confirming the participants informed consent, separately from the practitioners performing the ablation procedure. Participants will be reassured that participation in the study is voluntary, and will have no effect on the ablation procedure or waiting time prior to procedure.
