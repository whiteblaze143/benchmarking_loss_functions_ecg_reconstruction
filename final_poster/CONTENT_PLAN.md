# Poster Content Plan

## Conference message

Point-wise accuracy is necessary but not sufficient. MSE anchors voltage
calibration, correlation improves morphology when calibrated, and the best
loss combination depends on architecture and endpoint.

## Reading order

1. Define the limited-lead reconstruction problem.
2. Explain the E/C/M/D objective term by term.
3. Establish the controlled 3 × 16 experimental design.
4. Show the complete 48-cell landscape.
5. Test full composite versus MSE-only on prespecified endpoints.
6. Show the correlation-without-calibration failure.
7. Report architecture-specific best cells and conditional effects.
8. Stress test under deterministic Gaussian/NSTDB noise.
9. Evaluate EchoNext, smartwatch, and Sunnybrook transfer.
10. End with diagnostic-quality evidence, limitations, and the practical rule.

## Visual hierarchy

- Hero statement and four scale numbers.
- Factorial atlas as the central evidence visual.
- Patient-cluster BCa forest plot as the inferential visual.
- Noise and external/device scorecards as transfer evidence.
- Representative traces as a qualitative audit.
- Tables only where exact design or summary numbers are more legible than a
  chart.

## Claim boundaries

- Test-set best masks are descriptive, not checkpoint-selection rules.
- A non-significant ECGFounder change is not equivalence or clinical safety.
- Sunnybrook superclass labels are Philips statement proxies.
- Smartwatch signals are simulator/device recordings.
- EchoNext diagnostic outputs retain unchanged tabular metadata.
- Confirmation seeds do not cover every MSE-off configuration.
