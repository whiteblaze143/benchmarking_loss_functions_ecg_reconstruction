Checkpoint counts document what is recoverable; they do not supply comparable test metrics. The spatial queue studied A0 through E1, exact-\Theta controls, capacity matching, and permuted geometry. Those comparisons are interpretable only when mask, seed, observed lead, training budget, and checkpoint selection match.

7.3 Claim hierarchy

  1. A0 → spatial encoding: does explicit lead identity add information?
  2. Panorama → relative geometry: does electrode relationship matter beyond an embedding label?
  3. True → permuted geometry: is any gain genuinely anatomical rather than extra capacity or conditioning?
  4. A0 versus E1: complementarity only. Current 1110000 evidence does not justify calling E1 the improved base architecture.

7.4 Required interpretation

A result that improves lead I but not lead II is not automatically unstable: the two observed leads carry different spatial information. It becomes a broad architecture claim only after replication across seeds and both observed leads. Permutation controls are essential because a learned positional pathway can help through capacity even when its geometry is physiologically wrong.

7.5 Missing evidence

  • A normalized, per-run metric table for every registered one-lead checkpoint.
  • Seed replication for promoted spatial comparisons.
  • Common clinical evaluation under a one-lead-specific protocol.
  • External delineation for promoted models, separate from development-time RDB supervision.
