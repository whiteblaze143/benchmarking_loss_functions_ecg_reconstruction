### Remaining Critical Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

| # | Weakness (why it matters) | Minimum fix to raise the bar for a top‑venue submission |
|---|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Limited external validation** – All results are reported on a single PTB‑XL split. Without showing that the gains generalize to other ECG corpora (different acquisition protocols, pathologies, or demographic mixes), the claim of a broadly useful anomaly detector remains tentative. | Evaluate the full model (and at least the strongest ablation) on **one additional, publicly available ECG dataset** (e.g., MIMIC‑III WaveForm, CODE‑15, or the Georgia 12‑Lead ECG challenge). Report AUROC, AUPRC, and the same downstream F1‑score metric, with statistical significance testing vs. M0 and the cNVAE baseline. |
| 2 | **Narrow baseline comparison** – The only strong baseline is a cNVAE generative model. Recent work in ECG anomaly detection includes self‑supervised transformers, contrastive predictive coding, One‑Class SVM/Isolation Forest on learned features, and Deep SVDD. Lack of these comparisons makes it hard to assess whether the proposed loss combination is truly state‑of‑the‑art. | Add **2‑3 additional baselines** that represent current best practices: (a) a self‑supervised transformer (e.g., ECG‑Transformer or TS‑TCC) fine‑tuned for anomaly detection, (b) a classic one‑class method (Isolation Forest or One‑Class SVM) applied to the same encoder features, and (c) Deep SVDD or a variational auto‑encoder with a standard ELBO loss. Report AUROC/AUPRC and compute DeLong p‑values vs. the full model. |
| 3 | **Incomplete uncertainty analysis** – While ECE = 0.042 shows good calibration, the paper only shows that dropping the top‑20 % uncertain samples improves downstream F1. No reliability diagram, Brier score, or analysis of *when* uncertainty fails (e.g., mis‑calibration under distribution shift) is provided. | Produce a **reliability diagram** (or calibration curve) for the M2 uncertainty estimates on both the validation and test splits, and report the **Brier score** alongside ECE. Additionally, test calibration under a mild shift (e.g., using a different lead subset or a small noise perturbation) to show robustness. |
| 4 | **Missing confidence intervals & multiple‑test correction** – DeLong p‑values are reported only for the full model vs. M0. No 95 % CIs for AUROC/AUPRC are shown, and the p‑values are not corrected for the several component‑ablation comparisons. | For every AUROC/AUPRC comparison (full vs. each ablation, full vs. baselines), report **bootstrap‑derived 95 % confidence intervals** and apply a **Bonferroni or Holm correction** across the family of tests. Adjusted p‑values should still be < 0.01 to retain significance. |
| 5 | **Limited ablation on architecture/hyperparameters** – The factorial ablation only varies loss terms. It does not show sensitivity to latent dimensionality, encoder depth, or the weighting coefficients of the four loss terms (if they are fixed). | Conduct a **small but focused ablation** on (a) latent size (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64) and (b) one representative hyperparameter (e.g., the weight of the MMD term). | 6 | **Reproducibility artifacts not yet public** – The REPRODUCIBILITY.md file describes seeds, hyperparams, and compute budget, but the code and trained models are not released (or not linked). | Upload the **training/inference code, data‑split scripts, and the pretrained checkpoints** to a public repository (e.g., GitHub with a DOI via Zenodo). **Almost** – The manuscript has made substantial progress addressing the Round‑2 concerns (rigorous ablation, strong baseline, statistical testing, downstream utility, calibration, and reproducibility documentation).
