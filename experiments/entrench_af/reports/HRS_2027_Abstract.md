# HRS 2027 Structured Abstract

**Title:** Temporal Aggregation Alters Inferred Atrial Fibrillation Termination Dynamics: A Multicohort Holter Analysis

**Background:** Recent investigations of atrial fibrillation (AF) entrenchment have reconstructed episode durations from daily AF burden (`Spitaler et al., Heart Rhythm 2026`), whereas consensus statements distinguish cumulative AF burden from longest uninterrupted episode duration (`Doehner et al., Europace 2025`). However, cumulative AF exposure may comprise multiple discrete episodes separated by spontaneous terminations, potentially altering inferred duration-dependent termination dynamics.

**Objective:** To quantify the effect of daily AF burden aggregation on estimated termination dynamics and agreement between cumulative AF exposure and uninterrupted episode duration.

**Methods:** Annotated ambulatory ECG recordings from `IRIDIA-AF` (167 recordings, 152 paroxysmal AF patients), `SHDB-AF` (98 recordings, 72 AF patients), and `LTAFDB` (84 recordings, 83 patients) were retrospectively analyzed (8,540 raw AF intervals harmonized to 4,425 continuous runs, `g = 0 s`, zero synthetic imputation). After excluding 88 unknown-onset runs, shared-gamma-frailty Weibull models characterized termination hazard across 4,337 known-onset episodes (268 patients). Harmonized AF runs were compared against daily-burden reconstructions (`>=6 min/day`, `>=1,400 min` midnight bridging) on matched observation support across complete `24-h` recording windows (`>=86,000 s`; 2,195 runs vs. 210 daily episodes across 173 patients) using paired patient-clustered bootstrap resampling (`n = 300`). Duration-threshold disagreement across 383 eligible windows (267 patients), sensitivity to non-sinus rhythm continuity, `>=6-min` minimum duration, unfrailty Weibull fits, calendar-day `IRIDIA-AF` bridging, and `>=95%` / `>=99%` known-rhythm coverage were evaluated.

**Results:** Among 4,337 known-onset AF episodes from 268 patients, pooled frailty-adjusted Weibull shape was `k = 0.584` (95% CI `0.571–0.596`, LRT vs. constant hazard `p < 1e-18`), consistent with declining termination hazard (`IRIDIA-AF` `k = 0.661`, `SHDB-AF` PAF `k = 0.530`, `LTAFDB` `k = 0.591`). On matched observation support (2,195 native runs vs. 210 daily reconstructions, 173 patients), daily aggregation shifted frailty-adjusted Weibull shape from `k = 0.604` to `1.027` (paired `Δk = +0.423`, 95% CI `+0.283` to `+0.614`), lowered 1-h conditional termination from 5 min from `57.1%` to `20.0%` (`-37.1` percentage points, 95% CI `-44.2` to `-26.8`), and yielded a consistent unfrailty shift (`Δk = +0.582`, CI `+0.427` to `+0.814`). The paired shift persisted under non-sinus gap continuity (`Δk = +0.415`), `IRIDIA-AF` calendar-day reconstruction (`Δk = +0.606`, 68 episodes), and `>=95%` (`Δk = +0.423`) and `>=99%` (`Δk = +0.431`) known-rhythm coverage, while attenuating under a `>=6-min` minimum duration (`Δk = +0.162`, CI `-0.016` to `+0.393`). Across 383 complete `24-h` windows, among 142 windows with `>=6 h` of cumulative AF exposure, 25 (`17.6%`; `13.2%` under non-sinus continuity; `17.6%` at `>=95%` coverage; `18.0%` at `>=99%` coverage) contained no single annotated AF run of `>=6 h` (`IRIDIA-AF` `13.4%`, `SHDB-AF` PAF `19.0%`).

**Conclusion:** Daily AF aggregation attenuates inferred negative duration dependence and conceals episode fragmentation across Holter cohorts. Cumulative AF exposure and uninterrupted episode duration should be distinguished when interpreting AF termination dynamics.
