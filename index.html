Motivation & Clinical Context

Electrocardiogram (ECG) reconstruction aims to estimate a complete 12-lead ECG representation from a reduced subset of leads—such as a single wearable-like Lead I or a 3-lead input. Mean Squared Error (MSE) is a standard objective, but conditional averaging can attenuate morphology when the missing signal is not uniquely determined by the observed leads. This handbook tests that concern rather than assuming it.

The Regression-to-the-Mean Failure (Presacan et al., 2025)

In Communications Medicine (Nature Portfolio), Presacan et al. (2025) reported regression-to-the-mean behavior in ECG reconstruction trained with MSE. More generally, squared-error population risk targets a conditional expectation; when multiple missing-lead morphologies are compatible with the observed leads, averaging can attenuate uncommon amplitudes or shapes. Whether that occurs for a particular checkpoint and endpoint is measured here rather than assumed from the theorem or prior paper.

Potential failure modes include P/T delineation error, ST-segment attenuation, and QT/QTc drift. These are hypotheses requiring artifact-backed evaluation; the book does not infer patient harm from synthetic waveforms or global similarity metrics.

How to read this handbook

For current experiment status, start with the Live Experiment Observatory, then open either the three-lead queue and source monitor or the one-lead active/pending monitor. Completed one-lead screening, spatial, convergence, and external RDB results now have separate chapters, as do completed three-lead LUDB/RDB morphology, representation, and LUDB semi-supervised delineation studies. For a visual cross-experiment view, use the Deep ECG-AIM Evidence Dashboard, which shows matched mechanism effects, learning trajectories, external transport, boundary-specific failures, and paired linear-probe intervals without merging incompatible endpoints.

The book begins with evidence, not demonstrations. The opening real-data EDA audits PTB-XL and EchoNext in depth and now provides separate, stability-audited waveform UMAPs for every locally supported clinical cohort: PTB-XL, EchoNext, LUDB, ISP, Sunnybrook, Zhejiang, and RDB. The multi-dataset atlas establishes what the smaller morphology cohorts contribute, followed by a separate Zhejiang file-level audit. Only after those measurement contracts are visible does the PTB-XL preprocessing contract explain the content-pinned tensors and lead mapping.

The remaining parts follow the causal order of the study:

  1. Data, cohorts, and measurement — what was observed, how records were partitioned, and which claims each dataset can support.
  2. Reconstruction models and objectives — the architectures and loss terms applied to those inputs.
  3. Clinical and statistical evaluation — paired morphology, delineation, agreement, fairness, robustness, and uncertainty.
  4. Benchmark and external validation — the locked 48-cell evidence, coverage-limited seven-mask generations, completed external morphology, device transfer, and EchoNext classifier preservation.

This order is deliberate. A model ranking is uninterpretable until the reader knows the units, split boundary, repeated-patient structure, endpoint prevalence, and downstream measurement contract. Synthetic examples remain in the book as executable tutorials, but they are labeled as demonstrations and never substitute for cohort evidence.
